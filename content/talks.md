# talks

## environmental variability tunes the cognitive engines of culture

*second year talk · harvard psychology*

my quals talk to complete the Second Year Project. on an iterated learning experiment that sought to determine when we use different cultural encodings for social learning: those that rely on goals and causal knowledge of how to reach them, versus those that encode causally opaque procedures.

## computational principles of cultural evolution

*CogSci 2023 · sydney, australia*

[paper](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/78c4w10j) · [slides](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1WCAHXAugBF_DIHZK-DPZ--mluTEH624v/edit?usp=drive_link)

using an evolutionary model, we show that sophisticated social learning strategies may play a crucial role in insulating social learners from environmental volatility, thereby enabling cultural advances in one generation to crystallize and form the foundation for further progress in the next.

key takeaways:

- cultural evolutionary modeling is a useful tool
- the environment might shape how people learn from one another within and across generations
- specifically, in highly volatile environments, people might use model-based forms of social learning to transmit information that's resilient to environmental shocks

given at the 2023 Annual Meeting of the Cognitive Science Society at the ICC in Sydney, Australia.
